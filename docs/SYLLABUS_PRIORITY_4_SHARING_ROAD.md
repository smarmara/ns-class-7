## Cyclist coverage

### Safe passing

The general passing rules (pp. 57–59) apply to cyclists: signal intention,
sound horn, pass to the left, return right when clear. These are covered by
the existing `rules-pass-*` questions. The right-hook question
(rules-share-003) adds the cyclist-specific dimension of not passing and
cutting before a turn.

**COVERED**

### Passing distance

The Handbook does not specify a numeric passing distance for cyclists. However, the Motor Vehicle Act s.171B requires a driver passing a cyclist travelling in the same direction to leave at least one metre of open space. The Act also provides a centre-line exception: a driver may cross a line to pass a bicycle if the driver can do so safely.

**COVERED — CURRENT STATUTORY REQUIREMENT** — rules-share-009 now tests the one-metre passing distance and the centre-line exception, citing MVA s.171B. The Handbook does not state this figure, but current Nova Scotia law does.

### Passing when space is insufficient

rules-share-003 teaches: "if it is not safe to pass before turning, slow
down and move behind the cyclist before making the turn. Do not pass and
cut."

**COVERED**

### Turning across a cyclist

Two collision types are now covered:
- Right hook (rules-share-003) — same-direction turn across cyclist path
- Left cross (rules-share-008) — oncoming-direction turn across cyclist path

**COVERED**

### Intersections

rules-share-003 and rules-share-008 both set their scenarios at
intersections. The Handbook states "Most collisions involving bicyclists and
motorists occur at intersections" — this is the underlying principle both
questions test.

**COVERED**

### Dooring

rules-share-004 covers the "getting doored" collision: look behind for
approaching cyclists, do not open door unless safe.

**COVERED**

### Cyclist visibility

The Handbook's cyclist-visibility material (pp. 75: lights, reflectors,
reflective tape) is directed at cyclists. The motorist-facing implication —
cyclists may be hard to see at night — follows from general defensive-driving
principles already covered.

**CYCLIST-ONLY — EXCLUDED** (equipment details); motorist awareness covered
by general defensive-driving questions

### Road position / lateral movement

The Handbook tells cyclists to "use as much of the lane as required to be
safe from roadside hazards" (p. 72). The motorist-facing implication — don't
squeeze a cyclist who is moving laterally to avoid a hazard — follows from
rules-share-002 (cyclists have same rights and responsibilities).

**COVERED** (by the same-rights principle in rules-share-002)

## Current-law review

### Passing distance

The Handbook does not state a numeric passing distance for cyclists. However, the Motor Vehicle Act s.171B(1) requires a driver passing a cyclist travelling in the same direction to leave at least one metre of open space between the vehicle and the cyclist.

**Status**: COVERED — CURRENT STATUTORY REQUIREMENT — rules-share-009 tests the one-metre rule.

### Centre-line crossing

The Handbook does not address whether crossing a centre line is permitted when passing a cyclist. However, the Motor Vehicle Act s.171B(2) provides a specific exception: a driver may cross a line to pass a bicycle in accordance with subsection (1) if the driver can do so safely as required by Section 100.

**Status**: COVERED — CURRENT STATUTORY REQUIREMENT — rules-share-009 tests the centre-line exception.

### Dooring

The Handbook (p. 71) says: "When exiting your car, look behind you for
approaching bicyclists. Don't open your door unless it is safe to do so."
This is also stated in the parking section (p. 62): "Check for traffic
before opening your door."

**Status**: COVERED — rules-share-004 accurately reflects the Handbook.

## Genuine gaps

Two genuine gaps were identified and closed:

### Left cross collision

- **Concept**: Motorists turning left across oncoming traffic must watch for
  cyclists and wait for them to pass
- **Source**: ns-handbook-ch2 p. 71
- **Why it matters**: The Handbook identifies this as one of the three common
  car-bike collisions (alongside right hook and dooring). The right hook was
  already tested; the left cross was not.
- **Question added**: rules-share-008 (medium difficulty)

### One-metre passing distance

- **Concept**: Motor Vehicle Act s.171B requires at least one metre of open
  space when passing a cyclist; centre line may be crossed if safe
- **Source**: ns-mva s.171B(1), s.171B(2)
- **Why it matters**: The Handbook does not state the numeric figure, but
  current Nova Scotia law does. A Class 7 learner must know the legal
  requirement, not just general safe-passing advice.
- **Question added**: rules-share-009 (medium difficulty)
